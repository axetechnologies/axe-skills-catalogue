# Principles, with attribution notes

Note: this file records what is written about the work. What the work itself shows is in `what-the-work-shows.md`, which was built by looking at the archive's images and takes precedence where the two differ.

How to read this file: **Documented** means it appears in a source listed in `resources.md` and was checked while building this skill. **Widely attributed** means it is quoted often and credited to Rand, but the exact wording and source should be checked against his books before it is printed anywhere. **Applied** means it is this skill's own working rule, drawn from his approach.

## Documented

- **Form and function are integrated.** Visual communication joins the beautiful and the useful, each strengthening the other. (Summary of *Thoughts on Design* by The Marginalian, 2014.)
- **A symbol is defined by what it does, not what it looks like.** Simplicity helps, but the test of a symbol is its work as a shared language between designer and viewer. (same source)
- **Repetition builds rhythm.** Repeated words, shapes or images create texture, movement and order; numbers give a printed piece rhythm and immediacy. (same source)
- **Asymmetry over symmetry.** Bilateral symmetry is too obvious to engage the mind; asymmetric arrangements satisfy because the viewer resolves them. (same source)
- **The creative process.** Analyse, interpret, formulate; then unify and simplify, removing the superfluous. Intuition and the audience's feelings are part of it. (same source)
- **Idealism.** Designing by committee, or dismissing aesthetics, diminishes the work and its maker; catering to bad taste perpetuates it. (same source)
- **Rand's practice of testing identities for survival under degradation** (blurred or mutilated reproduction) is reported in writing about his corporate work. This is the origin of the squint, small and fax tests here.
- **His range, from the paulrand.design archive index:** logos (for example ABC 1962, IBM 1956 and later bar versions, UPS 1961, Westinghouse 1960, Cummins 1962, NeXT 1986, Yale University Press 1985, Morningstar 1991, Enron 1996, Colorforms 1959, AIGA 1969, Playtex 1941, Maidenform 1949, Direction magazine 1938); logo presentation books; book covers (Knopf, Borzoi); posters (National Gallery of Art, Museum of Modern Art, Typomundus 20); magazine covers and ads (Esquire, Apparel Arts, Fortune, Glamour); packaging and labels (Schenley Gin, Coronet Brandy); brand characters (a snifter man, a waiter, a chef, a rabbit for Lee Hats, a cigar man); paintings, photography and stationery. The archive lists clients, years and categories; it is an index; the visual traits are recorded in `what-the-work-shows.md` from the images.
- **Books** (published): *Thoughts on Design* (1947, revised 1951 and 1970), *Paul Rand: A Designer's Art* (Yale University Press, 1985, 27 essays), *Design, Form, and Chaos* (1993), *From Lascaux to Brooklyn* (1996).

- "Design is so simple, that's why it's so complicated."
- "Simplicity is not the goal. It is the by-product of a good idea and modest expression."

## Applied (this skill's rules)

- **One idea, said once.** The central tool of the skill. Statable as a single sentence.
- **Visual pun test.** Replace one part of a familiar object with another part of the subject; keep the swap obvious.
- **Ten before one.** Make at least ten rough ideas in at least three families before choosing.
- **One human touch.** Keep a mark geometric except in one place (a drip, an italic, a loose stroke).
- **Three-second rule** for screens and pages: purpose clear at a glance, from a distance.
- **Limit contrasts.** Use two or three chosen contrasts per piece, strongly, rather than many weakly.
- **Tokens before components.** Every colour, size and duration comes from a named variable.

## How to use this in a critique

1. Ask what the one idea is. If the author cannot say it in a sentence, that is the first note.
2. Point at the thing that does no work and say what removing it would cost.
3. Run the tests and report which fail.
4. Name the contrast being used. If there is none, say so.
5. Suggest a single stronger move, not a list of tweaks.
